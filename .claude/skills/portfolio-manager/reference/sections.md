# Candidate review: what each section of text.md says

The reader is a portfolio manager deciding with money. He reads the abstract and the decision first, then the figures. Each section is one or two paragraphs, 3 to 6 lines each, and says what the tables and figures show, in order, with the numbers that matter. Refer to tables and figures by what they are ("the weight curve", "the stress table"): the PDF numbers them.

## abstract (6 to 9 lines)
The book in one sentence (its strategies, its Sharpe and volatility on the window). The candidate in one sentence (what it trades, that it passed its test, its Sharpe on this window). What it does at entry weight (book Sharpe now and with, diversification multiplier now and with) and at full weight. One sentence in bold: the share of improving histories the decision rests on, and the decision. Then the one or two findings the reader must not miss: a recent change in a market correlation, a suspect day, a weight that should change.

## book
How the book is sized (15% per strategy, equal risk by family, IDM, 10% target) and what entry weight means here (half its equal-risk weight, the others shrinking: give the numbers). How the candidate's Sharpe compares with each member's.

## weights
Two questions. At which weight does the candidate help: read the weight curve (where the sample Sharpe peaks, the share at entry and at full weight, the share at the smallest weight and the gain it buys: small weights are safer but add little). Should the current weights change: read the alternatives (dropping the weakest member, the candidate in its place) and say why the answer comes out as it does, usually the correlations between members. End with what the weights should be.

## performance
Current book against the book at entry weight: the Sharpe gain, whether the bootstrap ranges overlap, drawdown, worst month, tail loss. The years the candidate helped and cost (appendix table).

## diversification
The correlations between members first (are they independent?), then the candidate's. The rolling view: the range of each strategy's correlation with the rest, when the average pairwise correlation peaked. Any suspect day: its date, what moved, which statistics it distorts, and that it must be checked in the data. The candidate's share of the book's risk (mean and max), and who carries most of it.

## exposures
The regression: is the book a disguised market bet (alpha and its t, R2)? Then each strategy with a significant beta (bold in the table), above all the candidate: what it was really long or short over this window, and whether any return is left after the markets. Then the rolling correlations to equities and bitcoin: the full-window value, the range with dates, how often it is above 0.3, and the latest reading. A rising latest reading is the thing to watch: say so plainly.

## stress
How the book did across the episodes (how many positive), the biggest ones, and when the candidate helped or cost. Draw the pattern (for example: trend pays in long bear markets, not in crashes of a few days).

## decision
The marginal rule (passes or not, after the luck haircut), the shares at entry weight on the whole window and after the candidate's train, which one decides, the decision as the script wrote it, and what it means (paper: tracked without money, re-run each quarter; enter: at half weight; stays out: kept in the library). What would change it.

## needs
What the book lacks, in 2 to 4 lines the Ideator can use: the markets or premia missing, the conditions in which it loses (its worst episodes, the market it moves with lately).

## limits
What makes these numbers weaker than they look: members built or selected on the same years (in-sample), the comparison of weights is in-sample too, suspect days, imported returns without gross, a short window for the regressions.
