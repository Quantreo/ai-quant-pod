# Which papers to keep

The desk builds systematic strategies. A paper earns a download when it can give the desk a strategy, or a reusable brick of one, backed by a test on market data.

## Keep

- Proposes a tradable rule (a signal and a position) **and** tests it on real market data with reported performance.
- In scope first: FX, crypto, futures and multi-asset. Equities when the technique transfers to other markets.
- Plus points: costs included, out-of-sample test, results by sub-group, a stated economic mechanism.

## Maybe

- A brick rather than a strategy (sizing, risk, rebalancing, execution), tested on data.
- A survey or replication of a strategy family with results.
- In scope, but the abstract is too vague to judge: read the introduction.

## Reject

- About research methods in general, not a strategy. Example: a paper on the reliability of a backtest validation pipeline. Useful, but it gives no strategy.
- Pure theory, no test on market data.
- Forecasting papers that report only accuracy (RMSE, hit rate), with no trading rule or no portfolio returns.
- High-frequency trading and market making: out of the desk's scope.
- Option pricing, credit risk, bank regulation, corporate finance, descriptive studies with no trading rule.
